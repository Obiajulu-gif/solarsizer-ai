# Pitch deck outline (5 slides, PDF)

Each slide answers one or more of the judges' questions (handbook Section 6). Numbers are from the example shop
with placeholder prices; replace them with sourced figures before export.

## 1. The problem
- A Lagos shop spends about ₦25,000 a week on generator petrol because the grid fails daily.
- Solar is cheaper over time, but buying it is guesswork: too small, too big, no payback date.
- Root cause: no neutral, location-based way for a buyer to check a quote.
- Answers: *Who uses it? What fails today, and why?*

## 2. SolarSizer AI
- Type what you power in plain words. Get the inverter, battery and panels for your city, the cost in naira,
  the payback month, a monthly payment option, and the petrol, CO₂ and money saved each year.
- AI only reads the list; tested formulas do the maths. Works offline after the first visit. Share on WhatsApp.
- Screenshot of the result screen.

## 3. Evidence: Brief 7 metric
- Example shop: about 1,300 litres of petrol and 3.0 t CO₂ avoided a year, ₦1.3M saved a year,
  about ₦4.07M kept over 5 years.
- Comparison with real installer quotes (collected during the sprint).
- What we built this weekend (from `CHANGELOG.md`).
- Answers: *What did you build this weekend?*

## 4. Cost, who pays, and the 6–18 month plan
- System about ₦2.43M, or ₦243,000 down then about ₦115,629 a month for 24 months, against about ₦108,333 of
  petrol a month today.
- Free for shops and homes. Installers and pay-as-you-go providers pay per qualified request.
- Months 0–6 pilot with 50 shops; 6–12 first pay-as-you-go partner; 12–18 nationwide.
- Answers: *What does it cost? Who pays? What changes in 6–18 months?*

## 5. Scale, risk, team
- New country = city list + price table + currency. Kenya and Ghana next.
- Biggest risk: wrong prices. Test against real quotes now; monthly partner price updates later.
- Team names and roles, and what we need next: one installer partner and one pay-as-you-go partner.
- Answers: *How does it travel? What could go wrong?*
